Nine Gerrymandle boards with exactly one purple win

Every board here was searched exhaustively: all legal ways of cutting it were enumerated, and in each case precisely one of them puts purple ahead. No second answer, no near-equivalent answer.

The counts beside each map are the real search space, not estimates.

Districts. A district is a connected group of tiles holding exactly the stated number of houses. Tiles touch along hex edges.

Open ground. Tiles drawn with a dashed centre hold no house. A district may take them in — to reach around an obstacle or bridge a gap — or leave them out. They never count toward the house total, and each one can belong to at most one district. Open ground left over at the end is simply unassigned.

Winning. A party takes a district by holding strictly more houses in it than any other party. An even split leaves the district dead: nobody takes it. A party wins the election by taking more districts than every other party.

Boards with open ground

These five boards have open ground: tiles that are part of the map and can be taken into a district, but hold no house. They are stepping stones. A district can reach across them to pick up houses it could not otherwise touch, and it can also ignore them entirely.
